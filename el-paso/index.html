Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in El Paso

Most mid-season breakdowns start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are simple. Change your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and rinse the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up each spring and fall, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

The age of your equipment matters just as much. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts acting up, like rising energy bills, rooms that won't reach the setpoint, or one repair after another, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and is far less stressful than an emergency swap during the first heat wave of the year.

Minor issues don't stay minor for long. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a short, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can take the compressor with it, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and call at the first hint of trouble. Catching it early saves money and prevents a few miserable nights.

Typical Heating & Cooling Issues in El Paso

HVAC systems in this area face a familiar set of enemies. Hard-running summers push compressors and capacitors to their limits, and humidity and airborne grit slowly foul outdoor condenser coils, cutting efficiency years before the equipment should show its age. Dirty filters quietly starve systems of airflow, freezing evaporator coils in summer and overheating furnace heat exchangers in winter, damage that builds slowly and out of sight.

Our technicians also run into the full range of component failures in local homes: weak capacitors that leave the condenser humming without starting, contactors with pitted points, cracked igniters and dirty flame sensors that lock furnaces out on cold mornings, slow refrigerant leaks that leave the house lukewarm by afternoon, and blower motors worn down by years of restricted airflow. The area's older housing stock adds its own layer, undersized returns, leaky or crushed ductwork, and additions that no duct run ever reached.

Every one of these problems is fixable, and most are far cheaper to address early.
